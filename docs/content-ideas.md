## How to write these

**Answer the title in the first hundred words.** Someone searching "how much does a website cost" wants a number or a range, not three paragraphs of preamble. Give the honest answer immediately, then explain what moves it.

**Use the real portfolio.** Every one of these topics can point at something live. A post about mobile money can cite SchoolSpec. One about offline-first design can cite Yourcountant. That is the difference between a post anyone could write and one only this team can.

**Never invent a statistic.** If a number is not sourced, describe the mechanism instead. "An enquiry at midnight is answered at midnight" is stronger than a fabricated conversion percentage, and it cannot be challenged.

**One call to action, placed after the answer.** The inline block now on every post points at a live agent, which converts better than a form, because the reader tests the product rather than taking a claim on trust.

## Priority order

Start with 1, 2 and 3. Cost and comparison searches catch people with budget who are choosing between options, and number 3 is the clearest statement of the combined offer the homepage now leads with.
